Beyond the Waffle Cone

Finite algebraic certificates, local similarity, and the characterization of Euclidean length.

Living research preprint · Not peer reviewed · @@COUNT@@ registered claims · Exact source revision @@COMMIT@@

Abstract

Inspired by the trigonometric constructions of Ne’Kiya Jackson and Calcea Johnson, we replace the infinite waffle-cone sum with a finite affine intersection certificate. Two geometric bridges derive its sine relations from explicit length-and-area or local similarity assumptions. A normalized one-parameter projection test is equivalent to the quadratic length law under a calibrated homogeneous symmetric length interface. The algebra and stated conditional implications are checked in Lean 4.

Scope. Deriving the remaining local similarity premise from elementary synthetic geometry, completing the independent comparison with the published infinite geometric proof, and establishing mathematical priority are open. A kernel audit is not a proof of foundational independence or originality.

01 / A finite intersection

For 0 < a < b, let T=(0,b), Y=(a,0), Z=(−a,0). The apex X lies at Y−u(b,a)=Z−v(a,b). Equating coordinates gives ub−va=2a and ua=vb. Combining these without dividing by a length yields:

(a²+b²)u = 2ab(1+v)

Under the geometric bridge, the common sine satisfies s(1+v)=u and sc²=2ab. Substitution gives a²+b²=c². The area and local-AA variants justify those relations from different explicit interfaces (C04–C05).

02 / Local similarity as a length criterion

Let L be axially calibrated, positive away from zero, homogeneous, and symmetric under reflection and coordinate swap. For every x>0, put p=(1,0), q=(1,x), and t=1/(1+x²). The residual vector p−tq is perpendicular to q in the affine quarter-turn sense.

L(0,x)L(1,0) = L(p−tq)L(q)

Because p−tq=[x/(1+x²)](x,−1), the normalized test is equivalent to L(1,x)²=1+x². The full local-AA rule, normalized test, and quadratic law on all vectors are equivalent (C06–C07).

03 / Quantitative stability

Define D(x)=L(0,x)L(1,0)−L(p−tq)L(q). Without assuming AA, the exact defect is

D(x) = x/(1+x²) · [1+x²−L(1,x)²].

Thus |D(x)| ≤ εx if and only if |L(1,x)²−(1+x²)| ≤ ε(1+x²). If ε≥0 and the bound holds at every positive slope, homogeneity and symmetry extend it to every vector (C08–C10).

|L(a,b)²−(a²+b²)| ≤ ε(a²+b²).

The equivalence preserves the relative tolerance exactly. This is a squared-length bound. With an additional triangle inequality, a finite mesh yields a guarantee: if samples cover [0,1] within distance δ and each absolute squared-length error is at most ε, the global relative squared-length error is at most ε+6δ (C11). A sharper analysis using reflection and convex combinations reduces this to ε+2δ (C16). The absolute squared-length slope error is 2-Lipschitz on [0,1] (C19), and taxicab length proves that constant is optimal for that Lipschitz statement (C17). Exact agreement at every rational slope in (0,1] suffices for the quadratic law everywhere under the triangle inequality (C18).

05 / Prior art and open questions

Jackson and Johnson’s Five or Ten New Proofs of the Pythagorean Theorem (2024) is the primary inspiration. Compare Shene’s reconstruction, Kise, Uehara and Shinzato (2025), and generalized sine functions in normed planes. Finite trigonometric proofs already exist. The precise novelty of the certificates and characterization remains to be established.

Next: independent synthetic justification of the local projection criterion, comparison with classical norm characterizations, comparison with the published infinite construction and sharper finite-sample stability bounds.
